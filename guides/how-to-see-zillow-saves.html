How to see how many people saved a Zillow listing

Where Zillow shows a listing's views and saves, what to do when they're hidden, and how to read saves per day against Zillow's own benchmarks.

Buyer guide5 min readUpdated November 2026

Short answer: On most for-sale listings, Zillow shows the number of views and saves next to the time on Zillow, near the top of the listing details. Some listings hide them. The count alone says little: what tells you how hot a home is, is saves per day compared with similar listings.

A home with 12 saves and one with 142 saves can look the same in the search results, because the counts usually appear only once you open a listing. The 142-save home is the one buyers are competing for. The 12-save home may be where you have room to negotiate.

Where to find views and saves on a listing

Open the listing and look in the overview, near the price, beds and baths and the facts list. Zillow shows a short line with the time on Zillow, the number of views and the number of saves. The exact layout varies between the website and the app, and Zillow changes it from time to time.

When the counts are hidden

Some listings don’t show views or saves. When that happens you have two options:

  1. Ask the listing agent. Listing agents can see how their own listing is performing on Zillow. If you work with a buyer’s agent, they can ask.
  2. Check the page data. Technically minded readers can open the browser’s developer tools on the listing and search for favoriteCount (saves) and pageViewCount (views). These fields aren’t always present and can change.

What the numbers mean

Save counts don’t exist in a vacuum. 50 saves in a big metro means something different than 50 saves in a small town, and a listing that has been up for 60 days has had far longer to collect saves than one posted on Tuesday. What matters is the ratio of saves to time on market, compared with the other listings a buyer in that search would also be looking at.

Zillow’s own benchmarks (Zillow research reported in April 2025): listings averaging about 5 saves a day typically accepted an offer within a week, 10 or more a day were likely to sell above list price, and less than 1 a day sat for over a month. Use them as rough guides; your local market and price range matter.

Have the numbers already? The free Demand Score calculator turns saves, views and days on market into one 0 to 100 score with the breakdown.

Views are a weaker signal than saves. A view can be an accidental click; a save is a buyer deciding to keep the home in their list. A high view count with few saves usually means the photos or price are drawing clicks that don’t turn into interest.

Why saves per day beats the total

Divide saves by days on market and compare that pace across the listings in the same search. Days on Zillow can restart when a home is relisted, so check the price history before you trust a short count.
