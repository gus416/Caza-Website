Privacy Policy & Terms of Use

Last updated: November 11, 2026 (Caza 2.11.0: membership and payments)

Summary. The Caza Chrome extension runs in your browser. It has no servers of its own, no Caza accounts and no advertising. It reads Zillow pages in your browser and does not send listing data to Caza. If you turn on rental comps inside Investor numbers, it also loads Zillow rentals searches in the background. If you buy a membership, your email and payment are handled by ExtensionPay and Stripe; Caza never sees your card. What you set and type stays in your browser, except the Chrome Sync records described below. Six things can reach anyone else, and each is stated plainly: (1) Google Maps links you click, (2) the map page you open with Show all properties on map, (3) the Chrome Sync records (a free-for-life marker and ExtPay.js’s records), (4) the Caza welcome and uninstall pages on this website, (5) usage counts, which are off unless you turn them on and are sent to Google Analytics from Caza’s background service worker, and (6) membership checks with ExtensionPay, only once you have opened checkout or log in (never for free-for-life installs; no listing data is sent). This website, usecaza.com, is separate and uses Google Analytics; see Website analytics below.

This page mostly describes the Caza Chrome extension. The website, usecaza.com (including the welcome and uninstall pages), uses analytics, described under Website analytics below.

What Caza reads

Caza reads listing data already loaded on the Zillow pages you visit (saves, views, days on market, price, size, property type, location, listing description, and similar fields) to calculate scores, comps and investor numbers. Listing descriptions are read on your device to find unit counts, rents and condition words, and on rental listings to find stated concessions (for example "1 month free") and lease length; the text itself is not stored, and it is not sent anywhere. From 2.10.0, Investor numbers also reads the listing's tax history (last bill and assessment), county, HOA fields and unit-count facts from the same page data, on your device.

On rental listings and Zillow's rentals search, Caza reads the asking rent, bedrooms, bathrooms, size, days listed, rent changes, required monthly fees and apartment-building floor-plan prices that the page shows. Rental comps use active for-rent listings only, never past leases. Caza does not read, show or filter anything about schools, crime, demographics, source of income or familial status.

Network requests

Caza's own requests go to Zillow, and none of them send listing data to Caza. The click-only and optional exceptions that go elsewhere are listed after the Zillow requests.

Requests to Zillow

  • Scores on results pages. To read saves and views, Caza loads the Zillow page of listings that are on screen or about one screen away, a few at a time and at least 300 ms apart. Listings read in the last 24 hours are not loaded again. If Zillow answers with an access-denied or rate-limit response, Caza stops these requests for 10 minutes. Caza never interacts with a "confirm you're a person" check.
  • Sales comps. Only when you click Sales comps (on a listing, or View Sales Comps in a score tooltip), Caza opens Zillow's own Recently Sold search page in a new tab, the same page you could open yourself, and reads the sold homes that page shows. Widen the distance and Older sales are another click that loads a wider Recently Sold search page.
  • Rental comps. Only when you click Rental comps (on a listing, a rental listing or an apartment building page), View Rental Comps on a for-rent search result card, or Find asking rents in the popup's Find rents for a unit, Caza opens Zillow's own rentals search page around that place in a new tab, the same page you could open yourself, and reads the active rentals that page shows. Picking a different radius (0.5, 1, 2 or 3 mi; it starts at 1 mi) or widening the search is another click that loads that rentals search page. The listing itself and other units in its building are never used as comps.
  • Rental comps inside Investor numbers (off unless you turn it on). Since 2.10.0, with "Show rental comps inside Investor numbers" on in the Caza popup, opening Investor numbers on a listing makes Caza load, in the background, the same Zillow rentals search page that Rental comps would open in a tab (around that listing, 1 mi, then 2 and 3 mi when a unit type has no asking rents; active rentals only) and read the asking rents it shows. One page per listing and unit-type search, in the same queue as the score reads (a few at a time, at least 300 ms apart); a search read in the last 24 hours is not loaded again; an access-denied or rate-limit response stops all of Caza's background requests for 10 minutes; zillow.com only. With the setting off (the default), nothing is loaded until you click Rental comps.
  • Get details for these comps (for multifamily also Get unit mix for these comps). Only when you click it and confirm, Caza loads the Zillow listing pages of the comps shown, plus any sales left out only because their estimated unit count differs, at most 12, one at a time about 1.5 seconds apart, and stops at once if Zillow blocks a request. What it reads (unit count, unit mix, condition and sale-type words) is kept with the 24-hour listing cache below.

Comps and PDF reports also show listing photos. Your browser loads them directly from Zillow's image servers (zillowstatic.com), using the photo links Zillow already shows on the listing; Caza adds nothing to those requests. Nothing is sent to Caza.

Exceptions, each started by you or optional

  • Google Maps links. When you click a Google Maps link in Caza, your browser opens a Google Maps search for that property's address. Google then receives the address and handles it under its own privacy policy. Nothing is sent unless you click.
  • Show all properties on map. When you click it, Caza opens usecaza.com/map in a new tab with the comps in the part of the link after the # sign. Browsers do not send that part to our server. Map tiles load from tiles.openfreemap.org, which sees your IP address and the map area you view. The map page has no analytics.
  • Chrome Sync records. When Caza is updated from a version before 2.11.0, Chrome stores one small free-for-life marker, caza_gf, in your synced extension data. ExtPay.js, the membership library bundled in Caza, also keeps its own small records there; the one that includes your email is written only after you open checkout or log in. See Chrome Sync below. Chrome Sync is run by Google under your Chrome settings.
  • Membership (ExtensionPay, only after you click Join or Log in). Checkout and log in open on extensionpay.com, and Caza then asks ExtensionPay whether you have a membership. No listing data is sent. Free-for-life installs never contact ExtensionPay. See Membership and payments.
  • Welcome and uninstall pages. When Caza is installed it opens usecaza.com/start once (nothing opens on updates), and when it is uninstalled Chrome opens usecaza.com/bye. The link carries only Caza's version number, never listings, IDs or anything about your use. These are pages on this website, covered by Website analytics below. The uninstall survey uses Netlify Forms.
  • Usage counts. Off unless you turn them on. See Opt-in usage counts below.

Permissions

  • storage: to keep the settings above on your device (and the Chrome Sync records above).
  • Access to https://www.zillow.com/*: to show Caza on Zillow pages and make the zillow.com requests described above.

Caza 2.11.0 adds no permission. Caza does not use the tabs permission and cannot read any other website; it adds no content script on extensionpay.com. Its background service worker records the free-for-life status above when Caza is installed or updated, opens the welcome page on install, sets the feedback page Chrome opens on removal, keeps the monthly report count above, opens ExtensionPay’s checkout, log-in and manage pages when you click them, asks ExtensionPay for your membership status (only after you opened checkout or log in), lets Caza use session-only storage when the browser starts, and sends usage counts only if you turned them on.

Opt-in usage counts (version 2.9 and later)

Usage counts are off unless you turn them on, in the Caza popup or on a one-time card shown on Zillow. When on, Caza sends the name of a Caza action and, for some, a short code. The actions are: active on a day, score seen, Sales comps clicked (where: panel / tooltip / card), comps shown, comps could not be built (why: bot check / expired / off-centre map / no results), report opened (sales / rental), listings PDF exported, summary copied (sales / rental), Rental comps clicked, rental comps shown, investor numbers opened (panel / comps / rental), first-run card confirmed, Rate Caza clicked, usage counts turned on, and, from 2.11.0, the free-report allowance nearly used and used up (sales / rental), the membership card shown, Join clicked (monthly / yearly) and Log in clicked, with a random ID, to Google Analytics. Each count also carries Caza's version, a membership-status code (free for life, member, or free), whether the free-for-life marker is set, and how long you've had Caza in coarse buckets (0, 1, 2–7, 8–30, 31+ days). Never your email or anything about your payment. It does not send addresses, prices, listing IDs or page links, and the ID is not your name. Turn them off at any time and the random ID is deleted from your browser.

If you uninstall, Caza opens a short, voluntary feedback page on this website. It asks for no personal data: one multiple-choice reason, an optional free-text box and the Caza version number. Whatever you type in the box is stored as written with Netlify Forms, so please leave personal details out.

Membership and payments

Since 2.11.0, Demand Score, sorting, hover cards and Investor numbers on the listing (including the optional rental comps inside Investor numbers) are free. Free installs get 3 sales comps reports and 3 rental comps reports a month, counted separately (one per listing or address, counted only when results are shown; Find rents for a unit counts as rental comps); a membership ($11.99 a month or $99 a year) makes both unlimited. Installs that existed before 2.11.0 are free for life, with no limit. See pricing.

  • Seller. Caza, not Google, sells the membership. Payment is processed by ExtensionPay (extensionpay.com) on Stripe. Caza never receives or stores your card details. See the terms of sale.
  • What ExtensionPay and Stripe receive. At checkout: your email and payment details, entered on their pages. When Caza checks your membership: the random ExtensionPay key above, plus the usual request information (such as your IP address). No listings, addresses, listing numbers, prices or anything you typed in Caza.
  • When Caza contacts ExtensionPay. Never before you click Join or Log in (the library has no key until then), and never for free-for-life installs. After that: when Chrome starts and when you open the Caza popup or a Zillow page (at most every 6 hours for members, 12 hours otherwise, counted from the last successful check, so while checks fail Caza asks again at each of these until one succeeds), when you reach the free limit, for a few minutes after you open checkout, log in or the manage page, when you come back to a Zillow tab within 30 minutes of that or while the membership card is open there, and when you click Refresh.
  • If ExtensionPay can’t be reached. A member’s last confirmed status is trusted for 48 hours after the last successful check. After that, Caza says it couldn’t confirm the membership and the free limit applies until a check succeeds.
  • Restore. If you reinstall Caza or use another browser, click “Already paid? Log in” and use the email you paid with. Free-for-life status is not restored after removing Caza, except where Chrome Sync still carries the marker above.
  • Cancel or manage. “Manage membership” in the Caza popup opens ExtensionPay’s page. Terms of sale and refunds: usecaza.com/terms.

No advertising, no tracking across websites, no data sale. The "Rate Caza" button opens the Chrome Web Store review page only when you click it. Links in the rental comps drawer (the FTC's advice on rental listings, and official rent-cap sources for California, Santa Ana and Los Angeles) open those sites only when you click them.

Limited Use. Caza's use of information it reads from Zillow pages or that you type adheres to the Chrome Web Store User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. Caza uses this information only to provide its single purpose: showing Demand Score, comps and investor numbers on Zillow. Caza does not send listing data or anything you type to Caza, and no person at Caza can read it. The only times it appears outside your device are when you click a link that carries it (an address in a Google Maps link, or the details in the usecaza.com/map link described above) or use a Zillow page you asked for. The optional usage counts (which contain no listing data, and are off unless you turn them on) are used only to measure and improve Caza's features. Your email and membership status (handled by ExtensionPay and Stripe) are used only to provide the membership. Caza does not use or sell any of this information for advertising, personalized or re-targeted ads, creditworthiness or lending.

Website analytics (usecaza.com)

This section is about this website, including the welcome page (usecaza.com/start) and uninstall page (usecaza.com/bye) that the extension opens. The extension itself sends analytics only if you turn on the opt-in usage counts described above.

The comps map page (usecaza.com/map) has no analytics. The comp list travels in the part of the link after the # sign, which browsers don't send to our server; the map images come from OpenFreeMap.

usecaza.com uses Google Analytics to count visits, pages viewed, where visitors came from, and clicks and interactions such as store links, buttons, menus, videos, FAQ answers and calculator use. With each of these, Google receives the page address (including any tracking tags in the link, such as which site or post sent you), your browser and device type, the site that linked you here, and your IP address, which Google uses for an approximate location and does not store. Google Signals and ad personalization are turned off, and we don’t use the data for advertising.

The calculators run in your browser. The numbers you type into them are not sent to us or to Google; analytics only records that a calculator was used. Google Analytics sets cookies (_ga and _ga_*) in your browser to tell visits apart. For visitors in the EEA, UK and Switzerland, those cookies are off by default, so your visits are not told apart or linked together; Google may still receive a cookieless count of pages viewed.

Learn how Google handles this data in Google’s privacy policy and how Google uses information from sites that use its services. To opt out on all websites, install the Google Analytics opt-out browser add-on.
