The state estimate leans toward a rental or non-homestead bill. If you will live in the home and claim a homestead exemption, your bill may be lower. Your county’s rate, exemptions and any local assessments will differ. Confirm with your county assessor or tax collector. Nothing you enter leaves your browser.

Why the listing’s tax is usually not yours

The tax history on a listing shows what the current owner paid. That bill reflects their purchase price, how long they have owned the home and the exemptions they claim. In states that reassess on a sale, such as California, Florida and Texas, your bill is built from your price, so it can be well above theirs.

In a few states, such as Arizona and Oregon, the assessment carries across a sale, and the listing’s number is a better guide than a percent of price. Homestead savings generally belong to the seller and do not transfer.

How this estimate works

The tool multiplies your price by a state-level effective rate, adds any special assessments you enter, and compares the total with the seller’s bill. A local rate you type in replaces the state rate. Read it as a starting point for your budget, not a quote.
